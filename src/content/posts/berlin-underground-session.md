The Berlin underground session took place in a low‑ceilinged basement, more concrete than decoration, with a sound system that felt almost too powerful for the room. Cami leaned into the space immediately, opening with hypnotic Afro‑Latin drum workouts and stripped‑back dub versions that stretched across the subs. Dancers formed a loose circle, eyes closed, letting the percussion lead.

Midway through the night, the set shifted into darker, cosmic territory. Jazz‑influenced synth lines and heavy tom patterns collided with snippets of Spanish and Yoruba vocals, creating a dense, cinematic atmosphere. The lights stayed low and static; the movement came entirely from the records. Every small fader move landed like a new chapter in a long story.

For the final hour, she brought warmth back into the room with soulful Cuban ballads flipped into extended edits and joyous disco‑leaning tracks. People applauded between songs, not just at the end, grateful for a journey that felt both experimental and deeply human.
