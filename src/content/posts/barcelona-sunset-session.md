The rooftop in Barcelona was already painted gold when Cami started her sunset session. Palm trees framed the decks, the sea was visible in the distance, and the crowd arrived with sunglasses and slow smiles. She opened with breezy Brazilian jazz and mellow Caribbean cuts, the kind of records that feel like warm air on your skin.

As the sun sank lower, the tempo rose almost without anyone noticing. Gentle guitar lines gave way to rolling basslines, then to dubbed‑out disco and island boogie. People stopped talking and drifted toward the center of the floor, pulled in by cowbells, claves and echoing vocal hooks. Every new record felt like a slight change in the colour of the sky.

When the city lights came on, Cami steered into more ecstatic territory: uplifted gospel‑influenced house, broken‑beat experiments and radiant Cuban fusion. The final track faded out to cheers, but the rooftop energy lingered long after dark.
